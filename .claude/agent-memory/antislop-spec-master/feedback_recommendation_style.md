When escalating Open Questions, lead with a **firm recommendation** plus the
reasoning that makes it defensible, then list alternatives beneath it. Do not
present a neutral menu of equally-weighted options.

**Why:** on the 2026-08-09 `ubiquitous-language`-in-spec-master round, all five
Open Questions were answered with the recommended default, verbatim ("all your
recommended defaults"). The user also *expanded* my authority off the back of a
recommendation — I had flagged a timing risk (an unbuilt, unassigned issue could
be grabbed mid-flight) and explicitly declined to act because of a stated
boundary; the user lifted the boundary and told me to act on it. Recommendations
backed by a concrete named risk get acted on.

**How to apply:** in the Open Questions section, and when a briefing hands me a
proposed shape. Interrogate the shape rather than implementing it as given —
that same round, the request's proposed "first pass at a rough spec" phase turned
out to be unnecessary (existing phases already covered it), and the framing that
the request matched a previously-named "candidate for its own spec" was wrong on
inspection. Both were caught by reading cited line ranges rather than trusting
the paraphrase. When a boundary blocks something I judge important, say so
explicitly with the rationale rather than silently complying — the user may lift
it.
