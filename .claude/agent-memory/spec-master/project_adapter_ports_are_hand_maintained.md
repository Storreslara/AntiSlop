`adapters/codex/agents-md-fragment.md` and
`adapters/cursor/rules/persona-protocol.mdc` are **hand-maintained ports** of
`templates/persona-protocol.md`. `bin/cli.js` only *copies them into place*
during adaptation (`:1599-1604` for cursor, `:2034` for codex) — it never
**renders** their content from the template. `--force-render` regenerates the
`.claude/` mirrors and does nothing for these two.

**Why it matters:** a protocol change therefore needs an explicit hand-edit
step for both ports. Distinct from [[validate-sh-is-a-mirror-parity-check]],
which covers the `.claude/` mirrors that *are* generated — a spec touching
`templates/persona-protocol.md` needs BOTH: a `--force-render` regen AND a
hand-edit of the two adapter ports.

**What keeps them honest:** `tests/adapter-protocol-parity.test.js` asserts
**literal strings**, not a generic hash/parity check. Its `ESCALATION_PROBES`
array (`:60-69`) is the pattern — a list of exact substrings that must appear
in both ports. Its own comment records why: gh299 and gh300 "both landed in
these hand-adapted ports, and a header-only probe would pass on a port that
had silently dropped either one."

**How to apply:** when scoping a protocol change, treat the probe array as a
deliverable, not test incidentals — removing a concept means removing its
probes and adding probes for whatever replaced it, or the test keeps asserting
strings the protocol no longer contains (fails), or asserts nothing about the
new concept (vacuous — see [[verify-own-criteria-nonvacuous]]). Editing the
probe list and the two ports in the same unit is what makes a
`node --test tests/adapter-protocol-parity.test.js` criterion non-vacuous.
